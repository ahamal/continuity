The second agent

This is the second build. It writes a row for every combination of the facts in a move, so the rows in between exist. To answer, it lets every row that applies have a say.

How it works

Compared with the first agent, everything in blue is different:

  • Writes. A move on the grid is seven facts seen together: the move, x and y before, x and y after, and the change in each. The agent writes a row for every combination of them, 127 for each move, and counts how often it has seen those facts together. After one bump into the right edge, "going right at x = 9 changes nothing" exists as a row, along with 126 others.
  • Answers. Every row that applies has a say on what will change, and the row that has been right the largest share of the time wins. A tie goes to the row with more facts.
  • Explores. It makes the move it is most curious about. It stays curious about a move until a row that agrees with its answer has held five times.

Try it

Press Explore. Each bar on the grid is a move it now expects to do nothing, and almost all of them are wrong. Click a cell with a bar, and the knowledge base on the right shows the rows behind it.

It does worse than the first agent. The rows it needs are all there now, including the ones in between. Choosing between them by vote is what goes wrong.

What is missing

A vote treats rows that were seen together as if one followed from the other. Having only gone up from a cell, the agent holds "at this cell, x does not change", and then expects that of every move from that cell.

A vote also makes rivals of rows that belong together. "Going left takes one from x" is right nearly everywhere, and "going left at x = 0 changes nothing" is a specific case beneath it. In a vote they compete on their share, and a row seen once beats a row that has held twenty times out of twenty-one.

And the agent never finds out. It does not plan through a move it expects to do nothing, so a wrong row of that kind is never tested.
